# Phase 4 — Search Engine Setup Report

**For Dr Mudrakouski's review.** What has been set up so people can find the page. The technical
files are alongside this one; this explains them in plain English.

---

## What is in place

| What | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Page title** — "Pain Management Clinic Dublin \| Dr Mudrakouski" | The blue headline in Google results. Leads with what patients search for, then your name. |
| **Page description** | The grey summary under the headline. Mentions spinal injections, radiofrequency ablation, PRP and joint injections, and invites a booking. |
| **Structured data** | A hidden, machine-readable summary telling Google you are a medical clinic and a physician: your specialty, address, phone, opening hours, Beaumont affiliation, fellowships, and your IMC registration number. This is how Google builds the information panel beside search results. |
| **Individual headings** | Each of the nine conditions and seven treatments has its own heading, so the page can appear for a search about any one of them. |
| **Sitemap** | A file listing your page so search engines find it immediately rather than waiting to stumble on it. |
| **robots.txt** | Tells search engines they are welcome to list the page, and points them at the sitemap. |
| **Sharing preview** | When the link is sent by WhatsApp, email or social media, it shows a proper title, description and image rather than a bare link. |
| **Web address** | Everything is now wired to **alexpainclinic.ie**. |
| **Mobile and speed** | Checked at phone, tablet and desktop sizes. No tracking scripts or heavy libraries, so the page loads quickly — which Google takes into account. |
| **Readability** | Text sizes and colour contrast were measured against accessibility standards, not judged by eye. Your patients skew older, so this matters more than usual. |

## What still needs doing before the page can rank

These are not optional — the page cannot appear in search results without them.

1. **Register alexpainclinic.ie and publish the site.** Nothing can be found until it is live.
2. **Create a Google Business Profile.** For searches like "pain clinic Dublin", Google shows a
   map with three clinics above the normal results, and most people click there. That listing is
   free and is driven by the Business Profile, not the website. **This will likely bring more
   enquiries, sooner, than anything else on this list.**
3. **Verify the site in Google Search Console** and submit the sitemap. This is also how we
   measure whether any of it is working.
4. **Connect the contact form** to a real email or booking service. It currently checks the
   details and confirms to the patient, but does not yet send anywhere.

## Two things to be aware of

**Your details must match everywhere.** Your name, address and phone number need to be identical
on the website, the Google Business Profile, and directory listings. We found that public
directories currently show a different suite number and road than the address you gave us — that
inconsistency works against you and should be settled before launch.

**Timing.** A brand new web address takes time. Typically: listed by Google within about two
weeks; appearing for your own name within a month; appearing for specific treatment searches over
two to six months. Broad terms like "pain clinic Dublin" are held by an established competitor and
would take considerably longer, if at all. Anyone promising faster is not being straight with you.

## How you will know it is working

Google Search Console shows how many people saw your page in results, how many clicked, and for
which searches. Impressions rise before positions improve — that is normal and is the first sign
things are moving.
