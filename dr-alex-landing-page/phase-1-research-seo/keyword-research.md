# Keyword Research — Dr. Alex Pain Management, Dublin

Research basis: manual SERP reasoning and on-page keyword mapping (no live rank-tracking tool was
run in this session — before launch, validate search volumes and difficulty with a tool such as
Google Keyword Planner, Ahrefs, or Semrush, and re-check quarterly).

## Primary Keyword

| Keyword | Search Intent | Targeted Section |
|---|---|---|
| pain management clinic Dublin | Commercial / local — user is looking for a clinic to book with, likely comparing 2–3 options. | Hero H1, `<title>`, meta description, Contact/Booking section |

## Secondary Keywords

| Keyword | Search Intent | Targeted Section |

| pain consultant Dublin | Commercial / local — user wants a named specialist (consultant-led care), often via GP referral. | About Dr. Alex, Hero subheading |

## Supporting Long-Tail / Condition & Treatment Keywords

These won't get their own pages (single-page site) but are targeted through on-page headings,
subheadings, and body copy in the Conditions and Treatments sections so the page can rank for
long-tail variants without diluting focus from the primary keyword.

## Notes on Search Intent Mix

- Most target terms are **commercial/local intent** (someone ready to book or get a referral),
  which is why the page leans on a single strong CTA ("Book a Consultation" + click-to-call)
  repeated in the header, hero, and contact section rather than top-of-funnel blog content.
- Condition and treatment long-tails are handled as on-page sections rather than separate URLs,
  per the single-page brief — each gets a distinct `<h3>` so it can still surface for its own
  query via Google's passage indexing.
- Local SEO signals (NAP consistency, `MedicalClinic`/`Physician` schema, Google Business Profile
  `sameAs` link) matter at least as much as on-page copy for these queries — see
  `phase-4-seo-setup/` for implementation.
