# Ireland Keyword Strategy — Where This Practice Can Actually Win

Research date: 2026-08-17. Domain confirmed as **alexpainclinic.ie**.

## Method, and its limits

This is **SERP observation**, not volume data. Live searches were run for Dr Mudrakouski's
treatment terms to see who currently ranks in Ireland and what kind of content fills the results.
That reveals competition and content gaps, which is the more actionable half.

**It does not give search volumes.** Nobody should sign off a strategy on this alone — run the
shortlist through Google Keyword Planner (free with an Ads account) or Ahrefs, filtered to
Ireland, before committing budget. Every "opportunity" below is a hypothesis about competition,
tested against real SERPs; the demand side is still unmeasured.

---

## Finding 1 — The biggest opportunity: Irish SERPs filled by foreign content

Searching **"radiofrequency ablation back pain Ireland"** returned almost entirely **US and UK**
sources: Mayo Clinic, Cleveland Clinic, Rothman Orthopaedics, topdoctors.co.uk, and a US pain
group. The only Irish results were a HIQA assessment PDF and one competitor clinic.

**Why this matters:** Google is falling back on foreign authority sites because there is very
little Irish content on the topic. An Irish page, on an `.ie` domain, written by a consultant who
performs the procedure, is exactly what those results are missing.

**This is the clearest available win.** It is also the least contested of his listed treatments.

| Priority terms | Why |
|---|---|
| radiofrequency ablation Ireland / Dublin | Irish SERP is thin — foreign sites are filling it |
| rhizotomy Ireland | Same gap, even thinner |
| facet joint injection Dublin | Related procedure, same gap |
| medial branch block Ireland | Highly specific, near-zero Irish competition |

## Finding 2 — Cost and insurance queries go unanswered by clinics

Searching for epidural and nerve root block pricing surfaced **a boards.ie forum thread** and an
**Irish Life Health "Pain Medicine Schedule of Benefits" PDF** — but no clinic answering the
question directly.

**Patients are asking about cost and finding forums.** That is a content vacuum, and it sits right
at the point of decision.

| Priority terms | Notes |
|---|---|
| pain injection cost Ireland | Currently answered by a forum thread |
| epidural steroid injection cost Dublin | Same |
| PRP injection cost Dublin | Observed range across Irish clinics: roughly €200–€500 |
| is PRP covered by VHI / Laya / Irish Life | Insurance-qualifier intent, very high commercial value |
| pain management without GP referral Ireland | Directly matches his confirmed self-referral policy |

**This maps perfectly onto the FAQ section already recommended** after the competitor review.
Cost, insurance cover, referral requirement and waiting time are the four questions patients
actually ask, and answering them plainly is both good service and good SEO. It is also the safest
kind of content to publish — factual, no efficacy claims, no regulatory exposure.

⚠️ Publishing actual prices is a commercial decision for Dr Mudrakouski, and once published it
must be kept accurate. An alternative that still captures the query: explain *how* fees work
(consultation vs. procedure, what insurance typically covers, that a quote is given before
treatment) without a fixed number.

## Finding 3 — PRP in Ireland is dominated by non-consultant clinics

The PRP results were led by cosmetic clinics, sports-injury clinics, physio-led injection
services and a regenerative-medicine brand. Consultant-delivered PRP barely appears.

**Dr Mudrakouski's differentiator is real and specific:** a consultant anaesthetist on the IMC
specialist register, FIPP-accredited, doing image-guided PRP in a hospital private clinic — versus
a cosmetic clinic offering the same injection. That distinction should be explicit in the copy,
because it is the honest reason a patient would choose him.

| Priority terms |
|---|
| PRP injection Dublin |
| PRP knee injection Dublin |
| platelet rich plasma Ireland |
| consultant-led PRP Dublin |
| prolotherapy Dublin |

## Recommended priority order

Ranked by winnability, not by volume — volume still needs validating.

1. **Radiofrequency ablation / rhizotomy.** Thin Irish SERP, he performs it, foreign sites are
   holding the positions. Best ratio of opportunity to effort.
2. **Cost, insurance and referral FAQs.** A live question nobody is answering, matched to his
   confirmed policies, and the safest content to publish.
3. **Consultant-led PRP.** Real demand, but crowded — win on the consultant/IMC/FIPP distinction
   rather than on the term alone.
4. **Facet joint and medial branch blocks.** Very specific, very low competition.
5. **Head terms** ("pain clinic Dublin", "pain management Dublin"). Contested by an established
   group with years of authority. Do not expect early movement here.

## What this means for the site

The single page covers the treatment terms through its `<h3>` headings, which is the right start.
But the findings above argue for two things the current build does not have:

- **The FAQ section** (already recommended) — now with a clear evidence-backed brief: cost,
  insurance cover, referral requirement, waiting time, what to expect. Add FAQ structured data.
- **Eventually, dedicated pages** for radiofrequency ablation and PRP. A single page cannot rank
  for everything, and these two have the best opportunity-to-competition ratio of anything here.
  This goes beyond the original single-page brief and is a phase-2 decision, not a change to make
  unilaterally.
